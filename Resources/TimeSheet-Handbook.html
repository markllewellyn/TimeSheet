Quick Start

Everyone

Sign in at the TimeSheet login page — either Sign in with Microsoft (your normal SVG IT account) or a local email/password if one was set up for you. First time on a local account? Use the one-time temporary password an Admin gave you to sign in — there's no separate step to set your own; if you want a different password later, ask an Admin to reset it again.

Signed in with Microsoft but seeing "Your account isn't set up in this app yet"? Your Microsoft account works, but no Admin has invited you to TimeSheet yet — ask one to add you from Admin ▾ → Staff. Sign out (far right of the header) always returns you to the login page, and if your session expires mid-task you're taken there too, with a "Your session has expired" message — just sign in again.

  1. Log your first entry. Go to Log Time (it's the page you land on) and click + Log Time, top right.
  2. Pick Client → Project in that order — the project list only shows what that client has. If a project has Entry Types configured, a third dropdown appears; otherwise it stays hidden.
  3. Enter hours and a description, then Log. It appears immediately at the top of your grid on Log Time.
Worth knowing on day one Every entry you save counts immediately — toward reports, invoicing, payroll, everywhere. Nothing you log is held back pending review. See Entry Flags for what actually happens if someone has a question about one of your entries.
§1

Log Time

Everyone

Your working grid — every entry you've logged, across every project you're assigned to, newest first. Search narrows by client or project name; nothing here is paginated away, so a broad grid genuinely holds your whole history.

Above the grid, summary cards show your Most Recent Day, Total Work Hours, Out of Office and Total Hours, plus Estimated Hours This Week — a quick sanity check that the week adds up before you move on.

Entry ID is the number to quote if you ever need to reference a specific entry to an Admin or PM — it's the same number used in Entry Flags search. A ⚑ Flagged entry has an open query on it (hover it for the reason) — it stays fully editable and counts everywhere as normal; a flag is never a block. If you're an Admin or a PM on that entry's project, the badge is also a link — clicking it jumps straight to that entry on the Entry Flags page. Edit / Duplicate / Delete disappear once an entry is approved for payroll, sent to payroll, or invoiced — at that point it's Locked, and the label tells you which: Locked (approved), Locked (sent to payroll) or Locked (invoiced). Spotted a mistake on a locked entry? Raise it with your PM or an Admin rather than logging a correcting entry.

Opening an already-saved entry for editing also shows an Attachments card at the bottom — upload receipts, screenshots, or any other supporting file, download or delete anything already there. Only available once the entry exists (a brand-new, not-yet-logged entry has nowhere to attach a file to yet). When logging a brand-new entry, Save & Attach (next to the regular Log button) saves it and takes you straight to that Attachments card in one step, instead of logging it, finding it again in the grid, and reopening it.

§2

Calendar

Everyone

Your Calendar shows the same entries as Log Time, laid out as a month grid — one colour per client, hours per day, a running month total top right. Filter to a single client to see just their pattern. Click a day to open a panel underneath listing that day's entries (client, project, description, W/H, O/H); click it again to close it. Useful for spotting a day you forgot to log — editing itself still happens on Log Time.

§3

Your Overview

Everyone

Your totals rolled up by project instead of by entry: work hours, out-of-office hours, amount due to payroll, and whether it's already been sent. Tick Sent to payroll only to see just what's settled. This is the fastest way to answer "how much have I actually logged against Everlast this period?" without counting rows by hand.

§4

Export

Everyone

Downloads a CSV of logged entries. Every filter defaults to "everything" — leave a dropdown alone to include that whole dimension. Last 7 Days and Last Calendar Month fill the date range for you; a regular user's export is scoped to their own entries automatically, so the Client / Project / User filters mainly matter for a PM or Admin pulling a wider slice.

Below the entry rows, a blank line then a short summary section lists Project Budget Hours and Project Hours Remaining once per project actually included in the export — the project's own all-time figures, same as the badge described under My Projects. Blank for a project with no Budget Hours set.

§5

Expenses & Contract Values

Everyone

The Expenses nav link lists every expense you've logged, with Edit and Delete on each line. + Expense (from there, or from Log Time) logs a new one against one of your assigned projects with an amount, currency, and description — separate from hours. A Rechargeable to Client checkbox (ticked by default) controls whether it should be passed on to the client or absorbed as an internal cost, editable at any time from the entry's own Edit page (it shows as the Billable column on the Expenses list). Not assigned to anything yet? The page tells you exactly who to ask and where (Clients → a client → Projects → a project → Assignments).

Once an expense is logged, Save & Attach (instead of Log) takes you straight to its Edit page to attach a receipt — or open it later from the Expenses list. Attachments can be added, downloaded, or removed at any time before the entry is invoiced.

Admins only A second tab, Contract (Admin only), logs a Contract Value instead — used only against non-invoiceable projects, and not tied to your own project assignments the way an expense is.
§6

Approvals

Project Manager

The Payroll Approvals page works in two stages, on two tabs. A PM sees entries scoped to the project(s) they manage; an Admin sees everything.

Approval Queue

Every entry not yet approved for payroll. Tick the ones you're approving, pick a Posting Batch (existing) or type a New Batch Name to group them, then Approve Selected. Approving is what locks each entry against further edits.

Ready for Payroll

Approved entries waiting to be sent on. Tick, then Send Selected to Payroll to hand them over to payroll.

Note Approving covers every entry type, not just out-of-hours — inherited from how this queue has always worked. Search filters by staff name, client, or project across whichever tab you're on.
§7

Entry Flags

Project Manager

A flag is a prompt to query an entry, never a gate. Flagging something never blocks it — the entry stays saved, stays editable, and keeps counting toward reports, invoicing and payroll exactly as before. Raise one when an entry needs a second look; clear it once that's settled.

Raising a flag

  1. Search for the entry under "Find an entry to flag" — results appear as you type, no need to press Enter. Matches by staff name, client, project, the entry's own description, or its Entry ID if you already have it. Type several words and each is matched independently, so it doesn't matter which of those fields each one lands in — e.g. "Sarah migration" finds it whether "Sarah" is the staff name and "migration" only appears in the project name, or the other way round. A numeric search matches the ID exactly, so it always finds it even if the entry is old.
  2. Click the matching row to select it — shows who, what project, and when.
  3. Add a note explaining the query, then Flag Entry.
Search scope A PM's search — and the flagged-entries list below it — is scoped to the project(s) they manage, same as Approvals. An Admin's isn't scoped at all.

§8

My Invoices

Project Manager

A read-only view of invoices that touch a project you manage — showing only your own project's lines from each one, never the whole client's invoice. If nothing's been invoiced against your project yet, the page says so plainly rather than showing an empty table. The link appears for every non-Admin, but only a project's nominated manager will ever see anything on it — for everyone else it simply reads "No invoices yet".

§9

My Projects

Project Manager

Only appears in the nav if you're the nominated manager for at least one project (or an Admin is acting on someone's behalf — it then shows that person's managed projects, if any). Lists every project you manage, each with an On Track badge — up to two pills, Hours X% and Cost X%, showing actual logged hours against that project's Budget Hours and actual cost against its Fixed Fee Amount. Green under 75%, amber to 100%, red over. A project with neither figure set shows a plain "No budget set" badge instead of a misleading 0%.

Breakdown, on each row, opens that project's all-time drill-down: three charts — hours by staff member, hours by entry type, and a monthly hours trend — each paired with the underlying table. For a Fixed Fee project there's no hourly rate to multiply by, so a "Recognized Revenue to date" figure is shown first — the fee, prorated by how much of the project's Budget Hours has been consumed — and each person's revenue is their share of it by hours (log a third of the hours, get a third of the recognized revenue). The per-person figures always add up to the Recognized Revenue figure — the same split Reports uses for a Fixed Fee project.

Admins see this too The same On Track badges appear as an extra column on the Admin Projects list (every project, not just ones you manage), and each project's own Edit page carries a fuller version — see Admin → Projects.
§10

Out-of-Hours Payroll

Admin

One row per month, showing the total out-of-hours pay due — click a month to see the breakdown by staff member. Each person's pay is their approved out-of-hours hours × their Out of Hours Cost on the date of each entry (see Costs, rates and roles). Months are generated automatically on the 1st, from out-of-hours entries that have been approved but not yet sent to payroll; Run Now builds last month straight away instead of waiting. This is a read-only summary — approving and sending entries to payroll still happens on Approvals.

Running a month again rebuilds it from scratch rather than adding to it, so if that month's entries have since been sent to payroll, they drop out and the total goes down. Treat it as a snapshot of what's waiting to be paid, taken before you send.

§11

Invoicing

Admin
  1. Pick a client and period, then Generate Draft. You get one line per logged Time & Materials entry (staff, date, description, hours, rate) and one per billable expense, grouped by project; a Fixed Fee project adds its own one-off fee as a single line. Anything already invoiced — an entry, an expense, or a project's Fixed Fee — is automatically excluded, so re-running this for an overlapping period can't double-bill it. Generating a second Draft for a period that overlaps an existing open Draft for the same client is blocked outright; finish or delete that Draft first.
  2. Review the Draft — adjust a single line's discount %, or use Discount whole project to apply one % across every line in that project's group at once, then type its invoice number into the Invoice number box.
  3. Finalize. The point of no return for what it covers: every Time & Materials entry and billable expense on it becomes locked — no longer editable, duplicable or deletable — and its project's Fixed Fee (if any) won't be added to a future invoice again.
  4. Download PDF — available once an invoice is Finalized (and still available after it's Voided). A Draft has no PDF yet; review it on screen.
Made a mistake? A Draft can simply be removed with Delete Draft — nothing on it was ever locked, so nothing needs unwinding. A Finalized invoice is instead voided with Void Invoice, with an optional typed Reason; both ask you to confirm first. A voided invoice shows who voided it, when, and why. Its record, real invoice number, and PDF all stay permanently (a correction trail, not an erasure) — that number can never be reused — and every entry, expense, and Fixed Fee it had locked becomes invoiceable again on the next Draft.
Monthly clients A client set to Monthly billing (their own Edit page → Billing Period) rolls forward automatically — a Draft generates itself once their current period closes. Run Billing Roll-Forward Now triggers that check immediately rather than waiting for the schedule. One-off clients are never touched by it; you always generate their Drafts by hand.

Reports

Admin

Pick a report, pick its Client (always required) and, for a project-scoped report, its Project too, then choose a range — Last 7 Days, Last Month, Last Year or Custom — and Run. Twelve report types, on four axes:

MeasureBy ClientBy ProjectBy RoleBy Team
TimeTime on ClientTime on ProjectTime on Project (by Role)Time on Project (by Team)
Cost to BusinessCost to Business on ClientCost to Business on ProjectCost to Business on Project (by Role)Cost to Business on Project (by Team)
Profit to BusinessProfit to Business on ClientProfit to Business on ProjectProfit to Business on Project (by Role)Profit to Business on Project (by Team)

The "by Role"/"by Team" reports group by each staff member's current job role/team, not whichever they held on the entry's own date — and bucket anyone with no role or team set under "Unassigned" rather than dropping their hours. Both are project-scoped only, not client-scoped, since a client can span Fixed Fee and Time & Materials projects whose revenue can't honestly be split by role or team.

On a Fixed Fee project, the Profit reports' summary Billed is the project's recognized revenue for the date range, and each user, role or team row's Billed is its share of that by hours (a note above the table says so). The rows always add up exactly to the summary, and each row's Profit is its share minus its real cost.

A project-scoped Time report also shows Budget Hours/Hours Remaining in its summary (the project's own all-time figures, same as My Projects's badge — not just what the selected date range covers, so these can look surprising next to a narrow range like Last 7 Days). Time on Client instead shows them per project in its breakdown table, since a whole client's "hours remaining" isn't one honest number across projects with different or no budgets.

§13

Admin

Admin

Eight screens under the Admin ▾ menu, the last item in the main nav bar — the one-time setup work rather than day-to-day workflow, which is why they're tucked away from the main nav.

Clients
Client records, reporting currency, billing period (One-off / Monthly), rate overrides for a specific person at that client. Deactivate / Reactivate from the list — an inactive client is left out of the Reports client picker, so reactivate it to report on it.
Projects
Per client; open one from the list with Edit / Rates. Payment model (Time & Materials / Fixed Project Cost — shown as "Fixed Cost" on the list), budget hours, project manager, Can Invoice flag, staff Assignments, Entry Types, and a Documents card for uploading supporting files (contracts, statements of work, anything worth keeping alongside the project). A separate Cost Exempt flag marks a project as pure internal hour-tracking (e.g. Training) — time logged against it carries no cost to the business, and staff can log hours there without needing a cost rate configured at all. The project list itself carries the same On Track badge and Breakdown link described under My Projects, for every project, not just ones you manage. Opening a project's own Edit page adds two more panels below the form: Budget & Cost Status (the same hours/cost-vs-budget figures as the badge, as progress bars — the cost bar only when a Fixed Fee Amount is set — with a View Breakdown link) and Estimated Cost / Profit — a forward-looking estimate, split both by role and by user, of what the project's Budget Hours will cost and earn once fully consumed, based on each assignee's allocated share of it.
Rate Cards
What a role bills at by default, or narrowed to one client or one project. Every rate is effective-dated — adding a new one never edits an old one, it just takes over from its start date on.
Roles
Job-function roles (Consultant, Senior Consultant, Director…) that a person's default rate and the by-role reports both key off.
Staff
Invite (including Find in tenant directory to look someone up in SVG IT's Microsoft directory), deactivate, promote to Admin, force a password reset, edit payroll number, and — per person — their Cost History (Hourly Cost and Out of Hours Cost, effective-dated; Add Cost starts a new one) plus every project assignment with its resolved rate and where that rate came from. A Team can be set alongside Job Role — filterable and reportable the same way, but purely a grouping with no billing significance of its own.
Teams
A plain staff grouping (e.g. Delivery Pod A, North Region) — a second reporting/filtering axis alongside Role, with no rate or billing significance. Same Add/Deactivate/Reactivate pattern as Roles.
Settings
Base reporting currency, default invoice month-end day, and the two firm-wide project budget-consumption thresholds that trigger a notification — Warning Threshold (%) and Alert Threshold (%).
Audit Log
Who changed what, when, and who they were impersonating at the time — filterable by user and date range. Covers timesheet entries (created, edited, duplicated, deleted, approved, sent to payroll), expenses, entry flags, password resets, and invoices: draft generated, discount changed (with the old and new percentage), finalized, draft deleted and voided. Invoice actions before 29 Sep 2026 weren't recorded.

Costs, rates and roles — how they fit together

Two separate things are priced on every entry: what SVG IT pays the person (their cost) and what the client is charged (the rate). The gap between the two is the profit you see on Reports.

Cost — what we payRate — what we charge
Where it's setStaff → Manage → Cost History (Hourly Cost and Out of Hours Cost)Rate Cards (by role), plus person-specific Rate Overrides on a client's Edit page
Varies byPerson only — the same on every client and projectPerson or role, optionally narrowed to one client or one project
FeedsCost and Profit reports, Estimated Cost, out-of-hours Out-of-Hours PayrollInvoicing, the Billed figure on Profit reports, "To Payroll" on the grid

Roles are what let rates be set once for many people: each staff member has a Job Role, and a Rate Card attaches a rate to that role, so everyone in it bills the same unless someone's been given an exception. To pick the rate for an entry, the app checks these five places in order and uses the first one it finds — the most specific always wins:

OrderA rate for…Set on
1This person on this projectNo screen yet — the app honours one if it exists, but a person-specific rate can currently only be added per client (step 2)
2This person at this clientClients → Edit → Rate Overrides
3Their role on this projectRate Cards, narrowed to a project
4Their role at this clientRate Cards, narrowed to a client
5Their role, everywhereRate Cards, the role's default

For example, if a consultant's role bills £120/h by default but they've been given a £110/h Rate Override at one client, their time at that client bills at £110/h (step 2 is found first) and everywhere else at £120/h (step 5). The Staff screen's Project Assignments panel shows each person's resolved rate per project and which of these steps it came from.

Worth knowing Costs and rates are both effective-dated, and an entry is priced with whatever applied on the entry's own date — then frozen, so a later change never reprices time already logged. A rate can carry a Discount %, taken off before invoicing. A Cost Exempt project still uses the rate but treats cost as zero. If none of the five steps has a rate (or the person has no cost in force on that date), the entry is refused with a "rate not configured" message rather than guessed at — set one up, making sure its Effective From is on or before the entry's date.

Acting on someone's behalf

The swap-arrows icon, top right, lets an Admin log time as another active staff member — for backfilling something they forgot, say. It only affects new entries created while it's active (a banner makes that unmistakable); it doesn't change what you can see or approve elsewhere in the app, and Revert to myself switches straight back.

§14

Glossary

Flag
A query on an entry, not a hold on it. See Entry Flags.
Posting Batch
A name given to a group of entries approved together on the same pass — lets payroll trace a whole batch back to one approval action.
Bill In: Current / Next
Set on an entry when logged (Time & Materials projects only). Current counts toward the invoice period the entry's date falls in, as normal. Next excludes it from that period and defers it to the following one — and only that one; skip a period entirely and a deferred entry needs re-flagging by hand.
Locked
An entry that can no longer be edited, duplicated, or deleted — because it's been approved for payroll, sent to payroll, or built into a finalized invoice. The Log Time grid always says which.
Voided (invoice)
A Finalized invoice that's been corrected rather than deleted — its record, invoice number, and PDF are kept permanently, but every entry, expense, and Fixed Fee it had locked becomes invoiceable again. A Draft, by contrast, has nothing locked yet and is simply deleted outright. See Invoicing.
On Track badge
A project's actual hours/cost measured against its Budget Hours/Fixed Fee Amount — green under 75%, amber to 100%, red over, or "No budget set" if neither figure is configured. Purely visual; unrelated to the Settings-configurable thresholds that trigger a budget notification. See My Projects.
Resolved Rate
The £/hr or $/hr actually charged on an entry, snapshotted at the moment it's logged. Resolution checks, in order: a rate for that person on that project, then for that person at that client, then for their role on that project, then for their role at that client, then the role's plain default. A later rate change never retroactively touches an entry already logged. See Costs, rates and roles.
